# Lecture+ A1 poster and Figma brand kit

## Goal

Prepare a compact, reusable brand kit for designing a vertical A1 poster in Figma. The kit must represent the existing Lecture+ product and its approved light Liquid Glass identity without inventing a new logo, product claim, or color system.

## Poster format

- Finished size: A1 portrait, 594 × 841 mm.
- Figma working frame: 2245 × 3179 px at 96 ppi, preserving the A-series proportion.
- Print setup: 3 mm bleed on every edge and a 20 mm internal safe area.
- Primary viewing distance: approximately 1–3 metres, so the title and main idea must remain understandable before smaller supporting text is read.

## Visual direction

Use a bright, calm academic interface with selective Liquid Glass depth:

- white and cool near-white background;
- restrained pale-blue ambient light rather than a saturated blue field;
- translucent panels with readable white fills, fine highlights, and soft shadows;
- dark navy typography for strong print contrast;
- blue reserved for the plus mark, important labels, process connections, and the primary call to action;
- generous empty space and no more than three major glass groups.

The poster should feel like the same product as the current Lecture+ website, but simplified for print. Blur and transparency must not be required for understanding; the design should still work if a printer reduces subtle effects.

## Brand assets

The deliverable will include:

- primary horizontal wordmark `Lecture+` for light backgrounds;
- reversed wordmark for dark or photographic backgrounds;
- monochrome black and white wordmarks;
- standalone circular plus mark in color, black, and white;
- existing square application icon cleaned and exported as an editable SVG;
- palette reference with HEX, RGB, and practical CMYK conversions;
- typography styles and usage notes;
- Liquid Glass tokens for Figma fills, strokes, shadows, blur, and corner radii;
- a concise poster content and layout guide.

All vector assets must use exact geometry for the plus mark rather than a font glyph, so it stays optically centered and sharp at any size.

## Typography

- Display and headings: Bricolage Grotesque, weights 700 and 600.
- Body, labels, and supporting information: Public Sans, weights 600, 500, and 400.
- Fallbacks may be used only if the fonts cannot be installed; the kit must provide the Google Fonts source names.

Recommended A1 hierarchy:

- title: 88–112 pt;
- section heading: 30–40 pt;
- key statement or process label: 22–28 pt;
- body: 18–22 pt;
- metadata and QR caption: 14–16 pt.

## Poster content hierarchy

The A1 poster should communicate the product in this order:

1. **Brand and promise** — Lecture+ and the concise idea “From what you learned to what comes next.”
2. **Problem** — lectures leave students with scattered recordings, slides, notes, and unclear next actions.
3. **Product flow** — upload or paste a lecture → receive a clear analysis → see likely next topics → test understanding → turn outcomes into tasks and reminders.
4. **Core value** — less catching up, more understanding; deadlines and facts remain checkable against course material.
5. **Product proof** — one clean interface screenshot or a simplified UI crop, not a collage of many screens.
6. **Call to action** — QR code plus the short site address `lecture-plus-ict.vercel.app`.

Do not overload the poster with technical architecture, database diagrams, login details, file limits, or long privacy copy. Those belong in a supporting presentation, not the primary poster.

## Layout

Use a 12-column grid with wide outer margins and three vertical zones:

- top 25%: wordmark, title, one-sentence product promise;
- middle 50%: five-step product flow and one product interface visual;
- bottom 25%: three concise benefits, QR code, URL, and a small accuracy disclaimer.

The signature visual is a large translucent glass pathway that connects the five learning stages. It should feel like one continuous learning journey rather than five unrelated cards.

## Print constraints

- Maintain strong contrast for all important text; never place body copy directly over the brightest ambient glow.
- Keep shadows subtle and neutral to avoid muddy CMYK output.
- Use effects sparingly: background blur may be simulated with translucent fills because print has no live backdrop.
- QR code requires a white quiet zone and must not be placed inside a translucent or gradient field.
- Export the final poster from Figma as PDF with bleed and fonts outlined or embedded according to the print shop's requirements.

## Verification

- Confirm every SVG imports into Figma with editable vectors and correct bounds.
- Check logo alignment at both small and large sizes.
- Verify the palette values and typography names against the current website.
- Inspect the poster at 100%, fit-to-screen, and thumbnail scale.
- Print an A4 reduction before A1 production to check hierarchy, contrast, and QR scanning.
